## About Me

Welcome! I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Wyoming and expect to complete my PhD in May 2027. I am an applied microeconomist at the intersection of behavioral, environmental, and public economics. I use theory, laboratory experiments, simulation, and applied econometrics to study how people evaluate individual and government investment when payoffs are uncertain, delayed, or hard to see. Most of my work focuses on prevention policies, especially why policies that mitigate natural disasters fail to garner public support even when they succeed.

I earned dual Bachelor of Science degrees in Mathematics and Economics from the University of Wyoming in May 2022 and a Master of Science in Economics in December 2024.

Outside of academia, I run [Tele-Hope](https://www.tele-hope.org/), a community-building nonprofit organization dedicated to suicide prevention in Wyoming. In my free time, I enjoy spending time with my dog, Wicket.

## Job Market Paper
<strong>The Intangibility of Risk Reduction under Heterogeneous Risk: Theory and Experimental Evidence</strong>

_Summary_: Prevention is chronically underfunded relative to disaster response, even when it yields higher expected returns. I propose that prevention suffers a curse of intangibility: its successes are hard to detect or to attribute to the actions that produced them. In a model where people judge an investment’s eﬃcacy from realized outcomes rather than their causes, an unattributable no-disaster leads to overinvestment, and an unattributable loss leads to underinvestment. A 2×2 laboratory experiment varying baseline risk and outcome attributability conﬁrms both predictions. Because probabilities are held ﬁxed across treatments, risk and ambiguity preferences cannot explain the pattern. Sustaining support for prevention may require rewarding predicted rather than realized outcomes. _Draft available upon request._
