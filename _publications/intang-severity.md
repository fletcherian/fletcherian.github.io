excerpt: 'The project examines when the structure of a risk environment, rather than withheld feedback, makes the benefits of protection intangible, and how that shapes collective investment in disaster risk reduction. Using a public-goods model and a 2×2 lab experiment with three-person groups, it crosses how protection works (preventing disasters vs. reducing their losses) with whether its effectiveness is certain. Holding the return to investment constant, it tests whether contributions decline when outcomes cannot reveal what protection achieved, separating the roles of non-events, uncertain efficacy, and shared responsibility in sustaining preventive behavior.'
