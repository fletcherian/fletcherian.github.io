with Faith Fatchen, Uditi Karna, Faisal Kattan, and [John List](https://voices.uchicago.edu/jlist/)

_Work in Progress_

This paper examines how early childhood development (ECD) investments evolve as economies grow. We develop a conceptual framework that emphasizes the interplay between survival and learning and predicts a shift from health-focused to education-focused investments as incomes rise and survival rates improve. The framework generates testable predictions about investment sequencing, non-linear spending patterns, threshold effects, and heterogeneity in treatment effects across development contexts. Integrating insights from the existing literature with new cross-country evidence, we present suggestive support for several predictions: pre-primary education spending rises sharply with income and healthcare quality; countries prioritize survival-oriented investments before expanding education; and health interventions deliver stronger impacts in low-income settings, whereas education interventions yield greater returns in higher-income contexts. While data limitations constrain comprehensive testing, our findings underscore the need for tailored investment strategies, prioritizing survival-focused interventions where basic health needs remain unmet and scaling up early education once foundational thresholds are met, rather than one-size-fits-all ECD policies.
